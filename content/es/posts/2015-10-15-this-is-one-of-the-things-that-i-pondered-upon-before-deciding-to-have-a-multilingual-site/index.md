This is one of the things that I pondered upon before deciding to have a multilingual site. As in your case I had an online presence from the early 2000's, but it was either English only and some in Spanish.

I later opened a blog in English on Wordpress and it was ok. I had an audience and and some traffic because I mostly wrote technical things. But I wanted something else. (Don’t we all).

A couple of years ago I moved to an <a href="http://andresmarrugo.net" class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor" data-href="http://andresmarrugo.net" rel="noopener" target="_blank">octopress site</a> (Jekyl). I had my custom domain with two octopress blogs on on the same site: one in <a href="http://andresmarrugo.net" class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor" data-href="http://andresmarrugo.net" rel="noopener" target="_blank">English</a> and the other in <a href="http://andresmarrugo.net/es" class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor" data-href="http://andresmarrugo.net/es" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Spanish</a>. The people that follow me in Spanish, most know me personally — it’s my native language. The English site is read by way more people, but most I don’t know who they are.

There you have it. It’s mostly about putting the words out there. Following the advice from Merlin Mann

> How do you know you should start a blog? Because people keep telling you to shut up.
